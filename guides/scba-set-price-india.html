What decides the price of an SCBA set?

1. Cylinder type: carbon vs steel

This is the biggest factor. A 6 L steel cylinder weighs around 9–11 kg, while a 6.8 L carbon composite cylinder weighs only about 4 kg. Carbon cylinders cost much more, but the lighter weight matters a lot for a person working in a confined space or climbing stairs in an emergency. That's why carbon sets (like the Dräger PA 91 Plus carbon or PSS 3000) sit at the top of the price range, and steel-cylinder sets at the bottom.

2. Brand

European and US brands — Dräger, Scott (3M), MSA — are trusted by many safety teams and are often specified in plant procedures. New imported sets from other manufacturers can give a modern carbon-cylinder set at a lower price.

3. New vs used / reconditioned

A brand-new branded set with original box and test certificate (for example, the new 3M Scott Sigma 2) costs more than a used set in good condition. Used branded sets are a practical choice for standby sets, training, mock drills and budget-conscious sites.

4. What's included

Always check whether the price includes the cylinder. Some sets are sold "without cylinder" (only backplate, mask, demand valve and reducer). A complete set should include: full-face mask, positive-pressure demand valve, pressure reducer with hoses, gauge with low-air whistle, backplate and harness, and a cylinder with valve.

5. Paperwork

Ask the supplier which documents come with the set — manufacturer test certificate, cylinder test/hydrotest details, and any approvals your site requires. Requirements differ between plants, so confirm what your safety department needs before you order.

How long does the air last?

A 6 L or 6.8 L cylinder at 300 bar typically gives about 40–50 minutes at a moderate work rate (around 40 litres of air per minute). Heavy work or fast breathing reduces this. For short emergency escape only, a 15-minute ELSA escape set (₹4,500–₹7,000) is far cheaper than a full SCBA.

Don't forget running costs

  • Refilling: cylinders must be refilled with clean breathing air after each use — either by a refilling service or your own breathing air compressor (₹95,000 – ₹2,75,000).
  • Hydrotest: cylinders need periodic re-testing (the re-test period is printed on the cylinder label, commonly up to 5 years).
  • Servicing: masks, seals and demand valves should be checked regularly, especially for sets kept in storage.
  • Spare cylinders: many sites keep one spare cylinder per set for quick change-over.

Is a carbon cylinder better than steel?

For the wearer, yes — a 6.8 L carbon cylinder weighs about 4 kg versus 9–11 kg for steel, so it is much easier to carry. Steel sets are cheaper and are fine for standby and training use.
